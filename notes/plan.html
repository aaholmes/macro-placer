The optimization framework

A real placer needs both a smooth local core and global topology changes. Three composable move types:

  1. Continuous repel — force-directed spreading (analytical core). Great locally, can't change topology or respect hard non-overlap by itself.
  2. Discrete swap — move a hard macro from a hot cell to a cold cell (global exploration; finally moves hard macros). Pure position-swap only stays legal for equal sizes — with 33× size variation it needs…
  3. Swap + correction — the hybrid that works: global jump, then objective-aware legalization pushes displaced neighbors aside.
The right repulsion force. Not generic 1/r² (that over-spreads and wastes wirelength). Our density/congestion terms are peak metrics (mean of top 10% / top 5% cells), so descend the gradient of a peak-emphasizing surrogate of our own score:
f = −∇[ WL_smooth (×1, attraction) + 0.5·Σ g(ρ) + 0.5·Σ g(cong) ]
with g convex & peak-weighting (e.g. ReLU(ρ−τ)² or a soft top-k / log-sum-exp). Force then evacuates a macro from a hotspot only while it's in one, spending minimum wirelength. Both grids are already computed in fast_eval, so ∇ is a cheap grid finite-difference — no Poisson solve needed. Loop: spread → legalize → swap → repeat.
