Macro Placement on the IBM/ICCAD04 Benchmarks

A differentiable global placer + greedy detailed placement, tuned by Bayesian search. Score = 1.0·wirelength + 0.5·density + 0.5·congestion (lower is better; zero macro overlap required).

What this is. Macro placement is an early step in chip physical design: positioning the large fixed blocks — memories and IP cores — on the die before the millions of small standard cells are placed around them. Because those blocks dominate wirelength, routability, and congestion, where they go is one of the highest-leverage and hardest steps to automate. This challenge scores a placer on 17 IBM/ICCAD04 benchmarks by the single cost above, requiring zero macro overlap; what follows is my solver and what I learned building it.

1.0155 avg score over 17 (mean ratio 0.692 vs reference) beats the reference on all 17 0 overlaps

Each benchmark comes with a reference macro placement (avg score 1.477). Placing from random initialization — not from that reference — my pipeline beats it on all 17 designs with zero overlaps, averaging 1.0155 (best-of-N over the tuned configs). A central design goal is generality: the placer optimizes any differentiable loss — wirelength, density, congestion, or a custom objective all drop into the same GPU engine unchanged.

Compute. A single candidate takes ~3–6 min on a desktop (measured: 15k-iteration GPU global stage ~80 s; CPU greedy polish 105 s on the smallest design, 308 s on the largest) — inside the challenge's 1-hour-per-benchmark limit. The 1.0155 figure is best-of-N (16–40 candidates/benchmark); at those per-candidate costs a GPU-batched, 16-core-parallel-polish harness fits it inside the limit too, though I generated the candidates across sessions rather than as one budgeted job. A single median candidate averages 1.105; the rest is multi-start.

Scope. The metric is soft-macro-dominated: co-optimizing the standard-cell clusters drives ~98% of the gain, while perturbing hard-macro positions moves the score ~0.2%. That is the right play for this proxy metric, but the number reflects soft-cell placement more than hard-macro placement — the challenge target is proxy cost; the downstream Tier-2 timing flow, which would weight the hard macros more, is out of scope here.

How the placer works

The pipeline has three stages. The first is continuous and gradient-based; the last is discrete and operates directly on the true score.

1 · Differentiable global placement

Macro centers are free 2-D coordinates optimized by gradient descent (Adam) against a smooth surrogate of the score — the key trick is that the real objective (Manhattan wirelength, top-k density, overlap) is non-differentiable, so I replace each term with a differentiable relaxation:

  • Wirelength → per-net log-sum-exp of pin coordinates, a smooth max that approaches true half-perimeter wirelength as a sharpness parameter gamma grows.
  • Density → cells splatted onto a grid; an overflow penalty pushes the grid toward a target fill so macros spread out instead of piling up.
  • Overlap is not penalized in the loss; it is removed afterward by a dedicated legalizer.

Qualitatively the optimization runs a homotopy schedule: it starts loose and weakly-constrained, lets the netlist's attractive forces gather connected macros, and only in the final phase ramps the wirelength and density weights up sharply to snap the layout into a compact, legal-ish arrangement. This "spread first, tighten late" order (controlled by ramp_p≈3.8) avoids the local minima that a fully-on objective falls into from the first step.

2 · Legalization

Three stages: a vectorized push-apart pass, a strictly-gated Gauss-Seidel cleanup that never worsens the score, and a relocate-to-empty finisher for anything still overlapping. Macros move as little as possible. Output: strict zero overlap on every one of the 17 benchmarks.

3 · Greedy detailed placement

The differentiable stage optimizes a surrogate; the legalizer then perturbs it. A greedy local search closes that gap by proposing single-macro moves and accepting only those that lower the true score (evaluated incrementally for speed). It touches soft macros only, so hard-macro legality is preserved. This stage does most of the absolute-quality work — which is also why the score is soft-macro-dominated.

Multi-start + best-of-N

Random-init variance is large, so each design is placed from many seeds and the best-scoring result is kept — worth ~6% on its own (see below), and the difference between the single-candidate 1.105 and the best-of-N 1.0155. Best-of-N saturates (log-diminishing), so past a few dozen candidates the gains flatten.

Why iters differs between tuning and deployment

The 5000 in the tuned config is an artifact of the search, not a finding. I capped iters at 5000 during Bayesian optimization purely for throughput — ~230 trials × 3 designs × seeds is only affordable with a short budget, and the cap let the search explore the shape of the loss (weights, schedule, sharpness) cheaply. The search duly pushed iters to its upper bound, which is the classic sign that the bound, not the optimum, is binding. So when deploying the tuned weights for real placements I tripled it to 15000. Sweeping it afterward on the three tuning designs (post-greedy score):

Tripling to 15 k is worth ~1.5–2% on the small and medium designs, at a diff-stage cost that scales roughly linearly with iters (~80 s at 15 k). It is not uniformly good: ibm17 gets slightly worse (1.185→1.213), the one large congestion-bound design in the sweep — consistent with the finding that the congestion-bound designs behave differently. Going to 30 k buys almost nothing over 15 k (≤1%), so 15 k is the knee. Caveat: these are single-seed numbers against a ~2% seed-noise floor, so the ibm17 regression and the 15 k→30 k deltas are inside the noise band; only the 5 k→15 k gain on ibm01/ibm09 is comfortably outside it.

What made the difference: multi-start beats a lucky seed

Single-seed scores carry ~2% noise, larger than the gap between good configurations. Ranking configs by a single seed is therefore unreliable — the search's apparent best (trial 95) was simply a lucky draw. Re-scoring the top five at best-of-16 reshuffled the order and promoted trial 221, which had ranked only 5th on a single seed, while lowering the score by ~6%. This is why the final pick is made under multi-seed evaluation, not by the search's single-seed leader. It is also why every experiment in the appendix is paired (identical seeds across arms, differenced per seed): un-paired comparisons at this effect size measure seed luck, not the intervention.

Where the remaining gap is

The unconstrained score sits ~7% above the leaderboard leaders (~0.9507 avg), and the gap concentrates on the hard, congestion-bound designs (ibm08/17/18). Two intuitive levers I can now rule out by paired experiment (both detailed in the appendix): a better congestion term in the global loss, and a congestion-directed detailed placer. Both are neutral-to-negative, because the score is soft-cell-dominated and greedy already minimizes true congestion directly.

What remains is structural. Congestion is 57% of the remaining cost at my best point and the term greedy reduces least — because it depends on discrete routing, not a smooth function of positions. Single-cell greedy at zero temperature plateaus against it for a specific mechanical reason: relieving a hot region requires moving groups of cells coherently, and any single move that vacates a congested cell lengthens its own nets and is rejected. The lever is therefore a detailed placer that makes coordinated, multi-cell moves (or runs under a temperature that accepts transient wirelength increases), so it can restructure the arrangement rather than locally polish it. That — not more hyperparameter tuning and not more congestion modeling — is where the remaining gap lives.

Appendix — tried but not used

The negative results constrain the design as much as the positive ones. Each was evaluated with paired experiments (identical seeds across arms, differencing per seed) to cancel the ~2–6% seed noise that otherwise swamps these effects.

Congestion surrogates in the global loss

The true congestion metric deposits, per net, one unit of routing demand along an L-route skeleton (a horizontal segment on the source row, a vertical segment on the sink column) into capacity-normalized H/V grids, box-blurs them, and reports abu₅ = the mean of the top 5% of cells. It is non-differentiable in the macro coordinates twice over — the routing is discrete, and the top-k mean is a sort. I built two differentiable surrogates for it:

  • RUDY — routing demand as a net's bounding-box footprint spread over its cells. Differentiable and cheap, but gameable: deposited demand scales as 1/bbox-area, so the optimizer lowers the surrogate by spreading nets while the true L-route congestion rises. Correlation with the true metric ≈ 0. Abandoned.
  • L-route surrogate — a faithful, non-gameable differentiable version of the real thing: a partition-of-unity soft row/column assignment (softrow) and a sigmoid-product segment occupancy (softseg) deposit demand on the L-route skeleton, and the top-5% mean becomes a bisection soft-top-k. It correlates 0.94–0.99 with true congestion — i.e. the surrogate itself works.

The L-route surrogate was then added to the global loss under four schedules: persistent, late (ramped on τ), congestion-first (congestion at t=0, WL/density ramped in afterward), and as a low-congestion warm-start (a congestion+spread pre-phase before the standard schedule). Paired isolation tests on the hard designs came back neutral-to-negative on the post-greedy score every time — e.g. the low-congestion warm-start: +0.09 score, 0/4 seeds, the warm-started descent lands in a worse basin. A "scatter" (anti-clustered) warm-start leaned slightly positive but sat inside the n=4 noise band.

Mechanism: greedy optimizes true congestion directly, so an explicit global term adds no capability the pipeline lacks, while corrupting the wirelength basin greedy starts from. Congestion falls out of good wirelength + spreading + exact-metric polish; forcing it earlier trades away wirelength for an arrangement greedy would have reached anyway. A good surrogate is not the same as a useful one — the L-route term is faithful to 0.94–0.99 and still doesn't help.

Congestion-directed detailed placement

A move operator that sends hot-cell macros toward cold grid cells sampled from the live congestion field — the discrete analogue of a congestion gradient — with acceptance still on the exact score. Paired against baseline greedy it was negative: teleport-to-cold moves lengthen the moved cell's own nets and get rejected, so they waste the fixed move budget. Zero-temperature single-cell descent cannot express the coordinated moves congestion relief actually needs — which is exactly what motivates the "coordinated moves" conclusion above.

Concurrent / interleaved global + detailed

Alternating chunks of Adam steps and greedy moves. Every confound-controlled variant, including SA-style acceptance and matched compute, never beats the phase-separated global-then-greedy pipeline. The two stages are genuinely separable: the global stage sets the coarse arrangement, greedy does the exact-metric detail.

Other

  • Electrostatic (FFT-Poisson) density field — the ePlace-style global density model. Matched the simpler overflow penalty; not worth the cost.
  • LapSum vs soft-max top-k — a differentiable exact-top-k (implicit-function gradient). Helped density-only runs (1.21→1.17) and is used inside the loss, but was not decisive.
  • Differentiable in-loss legalization + displacement penalty — resolving overlap inside the loss instead of with an external legalizer. It systematically over-spread the layout (~1.46 vs ~1.14); the external legalizer is better.
  • Spread-first only / cluster-first only schedules — the tuned back-loaded homotopy beats both fixed extremes.
  • SA and SA/greedy hybrids (greedy-until-N-accepts) — no gain over pure greedy at matched compute.
  • Hard-macro moves & connected-cluster swaps in local search — the score is almost insensitive to hard-macro perturbation on these designs (~0.2%); soft co-optimization dominates, so soft-only greedy is both faster and as good — the measurement behind the soft-macro scope note up top.
  • Discrete orientation (rotation/flip) optimization — negligible score effect; deferred. (The incremental evaluator supports Klein-4 flips regardless.)
  • Multi-start initialization variants (density-only, low-congestion, scatter) — see the warm-start result above.

Bottom line

A general, differentiable-loss placer whose tuned configuration transfers across designs: it beats the reference on all 17, averaging 1.105 for a single candidate and 1.0155 under best-of-N multi-start. The differentiable stage supplies a good starting basin (6.2% better than greedy-from-reference), and greedy detailed placement does most of the absolute-quality work — almost entirely through soft macros, which is why the score is soft-macro-dominated. The remaining ~7% to the leaderboard leaders is not a congestion-modeling problem: an explicit congestion term in the global loss and a congestion-directed greedy both proved neutral-to-negative in paired tests. Closing it needs a detailed placer that makes coordinated, multi-cell moves. A useful next measurement is the hard-macro-only score, which would isolate how much of the gain is hard-macro placement versus soft-cell clustering.
